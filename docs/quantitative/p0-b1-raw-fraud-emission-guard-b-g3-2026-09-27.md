## 1. Purpose

The final composed P0-B1 review found a structural evasion pattern: a client could safely consume an inventoried canonical-hold route while another exported tRPC route returned raw fraud fields. B-G1 proves handling of known hold-capable consumers; it cannot prove that unrelated public procedures do not emit raw fraud evidence.

B-G3 therefore inverts the question:

> Does any procedure exported through the actual `appRouter` expose stored fraud evidence, fraud classification, or fraud-derived numerics outside a reviewed canonical presentation path?

## 2. Detector and enforcement contract

`scripts/ci/verify-p0-b1-raw-fraud-emissions.mjs` resolves the exported `appRouter`, including identifier-backed procedure values, and fails closed if an exported procedure cannot be resolved to a trusted tRPC builder callback.

It follows source/provenance through direct expressions, object properties/spreads, arrays, ternaries, binary/logical forwarding (`??`, `||`, `&&`, and other binary forms), computed literal keys, local initializers, destructured aliases, local-helper return paths, shared database helpers, and opaque generic transforms such as `Object.assign`.

A verified canonical-hold helper may exclude **only its own returned branch**. A literal `status: "FRAUD_DECISION_WITHHELD"` is not trusted as a canonical hold, and a canonical-hold branch cannot suppress a raw sibling return.

For SQL, the verifier recognizes `.execute()` / `.query()` only where the receiver resolves through `getDb`, `getConn`, `get*Db`, or `get*Conn` **whose declaration is proved to originate in the database module (or a local wrapper of that module)**. Imported aliases, local aliases, dynamic real-database import wrappers, and arbitrary-name local wrappers are followed; a same-named local lookalike is not database provenance. It resolves direct and literal-computed method properties, unbound variable aliases, `.bind(...)` aliases, and `.call(...)` / `.apply(...)` invocations back to that original receiver; identically shaped lookalike receivers are not database provenance. It reads raw SQL only from literals, tagged templates, identifier aliases, logical/conditional statement aliases, or a direct/literal-computed `sql.raw(...)` builder/callable alias resolved to the installed **exact** `drizzle-orm` package. Direct return, local-helper return, logical/result aliases, assignment-forwarded results, and result-receiver transforms that remain opaque are quarantined as `UNRESOLVED_RAW_FRAUD_SQL_OUTPUT`.

### Exact quarantine enforcement

- Fingerprints bind procedure key, source path, line, column, and sorted detected field set with SHA-256.
- CI fails on additions, removals/moves, changed fields, malformed hashes, duplicates, and wildcard fingerprints.
- This is an **unremediated quarantine**, never an allow-list. The future zero-tolerance `assertNoRawFraudProcedureEmissions()` intentionally fails until remediation closes every candidate.
- The hosted KINGA Quality Gate runs B-G3 tests, and the existing workflow-shape verifier plus its own regressions prevent silent removal.
