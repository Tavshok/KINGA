# P0-B1 fraud and automated-decision gates

**Date:** 22 September 2026
**Status:** Rebased onto the merged P0-A-2, independent hardening, Publication Hardening II, and browser-containment baselines. Combined behavioral validation is complete; final independent re-review and merge-readiness validation remain pending. No merge or deployment authorization is implied.
**Author:** Manus AI

## Enforced boundary

P0-B1 restores the evidence-authority boundary for **fraud scoring and every current automated claim-decision sink**. The current Stage 8 inputs include raw visual measurements, collision-physics derivatives, vision-model photo-forensic probabilities, scenario-model results, contact-patch findings, and cross-engine comparisons. These are useful as descriptive observations for a reviewer, but none is a qualified governing fraud-decision source in P0.

The shared `FraudDecisionEligibility` contract is source-bound to the live Stage 7/8 snapshot. It permits only `ADVISORY` or `UNAVAILABLE` in P0-B1. A forged, omitted, stale, fallback, or structurally inconsistent decision is recomputed as unavailable. The contract cannot create a governing score, level, adverse finding, or low-risk pass.

> **P0-B1 rule:** An unavailable or advisory fraud result is not low risk, neutral risk, or evidence of fraud. It is a specific reason to withhold automated action and route the claim for manual review.

Every P0 hold contains an actionable reason. It states that automated authority is withheld, identifies the need for independently verifiable claim-linked fraud evidence, and requires a qualified automated-decision authority before automated scoring or routing can resume.

## Eligibility rules

`assessFraudDecisionEligibility()` uses three live-source facts: the current crush-depth decision, whether advisory evidence is present, and whether the Stage 8 output is fallback or degraded. A fallback or degraded source produces `UNAVAILABLE`; otherwise, current visual and model-derived evidence produces `ADVISORY`. The contract stores only a disposition, a reason code, an explanation, and required evidence. It never stores an advisory number.

`preserveOrFailClosedFraudDecisionEligibility()` validates the complete persisted shape and rebinds it to the live source snapshot. Any mismatch returns `P0_UNAVAILABLE_MISSING_OR_INCONSISTENT_DECISION`. `hasGoverningFraudDecisionEligibility()` deliberately returns `false` in P0-B1, even if an object claims to be governing. P1 or a later owner-authorized package must define a positive authority path before this can change.

## Scope boundaries

P0-B1 preserves independent qualitative or documentary evidence only as reviewer-visible context. It does not create a score from claim statements, EXIF/hash/date provenance, or historical metadata. Photo-manipulation and AI-generation probabilities are advisory-only. P0-B1 does not alter fraud-model calibration, visual-measurement qualification, collision reconstruction, cost authority, learning/reference-data authority, schema, migration, seed, staging, production, credentials, or deployment.

The Stage 9.5 CGI fraud-looking fields remain outside this implementation because they have no active current path into Stage 8 or an automated decision sink. This is retained as a no-consumer boundary rather than being rebuilt in P0-B1.

The first independent adversarial review blocked the package on five real bypasses: nested Stage 8 score-bearing evidence, a legacy Stage 10 `0/low` narrative, ungated routing endpoints, a fallback consistency score of `50`, and post-hold physics anomaly enrichment. The follow-up review then found three further live bypasses: post-Stage-8 cross-validation recomputation, `50/medium` timeout/catch recovery plus zero persistence, and an enrichment helper that did not fail closed when authority metadata was absent. A subsequent estate-wide review found direct legacy assessment mutation/snapshot paths, workflow routing/dispatch, reporting/analytics projections, completion-based history/fleet updates, portfolio score thresholds, cluster score derivation, and registered fraud-bearing command/output routes outside the central pipeline. The final source-audit remediation additionally fenced contradiction-validation and decision-trace APIs, historical replay result/action surfaces, generic response-normalisation adapters, driver propagation, continuous-learning labels, and a police speed-discrepancy output.

The final adversarial re-review found eight residual live paths: policy simulation; legacy assessment-PDF export; external assessment upload; vehicle-verification reporting; fraud-pattern learning and calibration; recovery-potential scoring; canonical report normalisation; and assessor report acceptance. P0-B1 now holds the public simulation, upload, PDF, report, learning, and calibration routes before raw data is consumed; removes fraud influence from recovery scoring; emits no normalised fraud score/level or fraud-threshold verdict; and drops accepted assessor-report fraud labels.

A subsequent re-review found four remaining bypass groups: a separately mounted multipart external-assessment upload path, the registered `claimReports` legacy PDF/snapshot/replay/email router, causal-plausibility recovery scoring, and the remaining calibration-drift/history learning endpoints. The processor itself now fails closed so future callers cannot reactivate raw model scoring. The mounted upload rejects after authentication but before multipart parsing, storage, LLM execution, or response publication. Each legacy claim-report command now fails closed before aggregation, snapshot write, replay, PDF generation, interactive retrieval, or email dispatch. Recovery automation returns an explicit manual-review hold before any database read or recovery-case write. Calibration drift and calibration history now hold before any historic fraud record is read.

The final adversarial checkpoint identified one additional active route, `predictiveAnalytics.getDriverFraudPropensity`, which read `drivers.lastFraudRiskScore`, generated a derived score, and published a high/medium/low classification. It now returns the same actionable hold before the database query; score, classification, factors, and data sources are null or empty. Regression coverage checks both the returned hold shape and the ordering before the prior database read.

The re-review of that repair found that `evaluateFastTrack()` returned its P0-B1 hold only after opening a database connection and validating tenant isolation, even though it is called by the enabled production post-assessment background path. The hold is now the first executable branch, before `getDb()`, tenant validation, configuration resolution, or audit logging. Its regression replaces `getDb()` with a throwing spy and proves the P0-held evaluation resolves to `MANUAL_REVIEW` without invoking the spy.

The final caller-level re-review found that the background caller itself still selected a claim tenant before it invoked the corrected evaluator. Its caller-level regression supplies a scheduling spy and proves a P0-held decision returns `WITHHELD`, emits the hold callback, and never schedules database work; a companion source-order assertion places the production scheduling gate before the tenant `db.select`. Final bypass regressions cover processor, mounted upload ordering, legacy report commands, recovery automation, calibration endpoints, predictive propensity, fast-track evaluator and caller ordering, historical normalisation, recovery-score invariance, and vehicle-verification report dispatch. ## P0-B1a authorization-ordering repair

P0-B1a addresses the authorization-ordering blocker discovered during the first P0-B1 merge-readiness attempt. It does not widen P0-B1 policy scope and it does not begin the separately approved P0-B1b field-level report-redaction work. The repair requires each legacy access-control path to complete its existing session, role, tenant, resource, and historical-record checks before a P0 fraud hold is returned.

Vehicle Passport now confirms a requesting session’s tenant, resolves the requested vehicle, and evaluates owner or qualifying insurer-invitation access before withholding claim history or fraud signals. The runtime regression proves that an invited insurer receives the withheld response only after this access path succeeds, while an unrelated tenant receives `FORBIDDEN`.

Administrative pipeline health now preserves its original administrative-role and tenant-session checks before returning its P0 hold. The direct-router regression exercises a non-admin session, a tenantless administrator, and an authorized tenant administrator. The first two receive `FORBIDDEN`, the last receives `PRECONDITION_FAILED`, and no path opens a database connection.

Historical replay now resolves every claim-specific historical claim in the caller’s tenant before withholding replay. The mixed-batch case validates a tenant-local identifier and then rejects a foreign identifier before it can return a hold. Decision trace similarly rejects a tenantless session before lookup and returns `null`, rather than a hold or trace, for a cross-tenant claim and assessment. All fixtures run only through the guarded `kinga_ci_test` runner, insert uniquely named owned records, and verify removal by captured identifiers.

The focused authorization matrix passed **4 files / 19 tests**. A fresh adversarial re-review returned **APPROVE**. It confirmed that no hold preempts required authorization, cross-tenant callers receive no hold or resource-existence signal, cleanup is fixture-owned, `git diff --check` is clean, and no changed-path diagnostic was introduced. The known repository-wide TypeScript diagnostics and the unavailable legacy ESLint configuration remain baseline/tooling conditions; neither is a P0-B1a finding.

## P0-B1b field-level report redaction

P0-B1b replaces the earlier all-or-nothing report holds only for reports that contain a mix of legitimate operational information and fraud fields. It preserves tenant-scoped vehicle, claim, quotation, cost, readiness, approval-rate, portfolio, operational, and audit content. It removes only fraud-specific scores, levels, probabilities, indicators, classifications, verdicts, and fraud-derived narratives, replacing them with one explicit marker: **Fraud Decision Withheld — Manual Review Required**. The marker identifies the missing evidence and the actions that would resolve the hold; it is not a generic quality banner.

`server/reporting/p0FraudPresentation.ts` provides the shared text/HTML marker and recursive payload redactor. The redactor removes fraud and risk-derived fields even when they occur in nested historical payloads, then attaches the actionable P0 decision record. `reportDefinitions.ts`, the claims-intelligence and forensic renderers, the legacy report router, canonical report payloads, and the assessment-PDF exporter use the redacted projection instead of returning a whole-document hold. Fraud-only report types remain withheld because no substantive non-fraud content can be separated safely.

The assessment PDF retains vehicle, damage, component, cost, and quote information. It replaces collision-physics and fraud decision sections with their respective evidence boundaries, so legacy `physicsAnalysis` and `fraudAnalysis` are not passed into the template. Cross-validation retains non-fraud comparison context but no longer labels a result as suspicious, computes a fraud risk value, or presents fraud indicators.

Focused redaction regression coverage passed **3 files / 15 tests** before the approved stale-fixture reconciliation. The new presentation suite proves nested fraud/risk fields are removed while non-fraud fields remain, the marker is actionable and contains no score/level/verdict, legacy PDF rendering with adversarial physics/fraud inputs contains neither numeric output, and canonical PDF input does not carry physics/fraud fields. The approved DOE and driver historical-score fixture updates then passed **3 files / 108 tests**. One scoped independent review of P0-B1b and combined merge-readiness validation remain required before a pull request can be opened.

## P0-A-2 rebase integration and authorization repair

P0-B1 was rebased onto the merged P0-A-2 collision-physics publication boundary without replacing its presentation helpers, canonical PDF adapter, LLM narrative hold, or collision-physics regressions. The combined report surfaces render the two independent actionable markers: P0-A-2 controls collision physics, while P0-B1 controls fraud automation. Neither marker supplies authority for the other domain.

The composed branch removes the legacy `fraudScoreAdjusted` presentation fallback and preserves the P0-B1 rule that unavailable or advisory fraud evidence is never formatted as a neutral, low, moderate, or threshold-capable value. Claim Assessment, Claims Intelligence, Forensic Decision, generic assessment PDF, report snapshots, interactive report projections, and report narratives retain independently supported non-fraud evidence while withholding fraud fields.

The final post-rebase source audit found that the model still calculated and exposed `fraudScoreAdjusted` before a renderer could suppress it. The model field, adjustment calculation, score-derived legacy review trigger, and fixture expectation are removed. The regression asserts that the forensic executive model does not expose the field and does not create a `fraud score 77/100 (threshold: 50)` trigger. Fraud presentation tests were also reconciled to consume the one shared P0 hold contract rather than an obsolete router-local alias; this preserves actionable wording without adding a score or a risk-level field.

A fresh independent review blocked the first composed integration on four live paths. The processor queue had ordered claims by historic fraud score and returned fraud score/level fields; it now uses chronological operational ordering and a defensive allow-list projection that strips accidental fraud fields and attaches the manual-review state. Geographic clustering had aggregated historic fraud classifications and scores into fraud rates and high-risk ordering; it now presents only tenant-scoped workload, incident type, and financial exposure plus the actionable fraud hold. The fast-track evaluator had returned the hold before claim/tenant validation; it now validates the resource and tenant before withholding automation, so foreign and nonexistent claims retain their normal error behavior. TRE v4 fraud-bearing simulation, certification, dashboard, and Trust API endpoints had held immediately after authentication; each now runs the applicable tenant and assessment check before returning the P0 hold.

The strengthened focused matrix passed **13 files / 87 tests** through the guarded `kinga_ci_test` runner. It includes behavioral regressions showing contradictory high/low historic fraud values cannot affect operational queue ordering or geographic workload output, fast-track returns its normal not-found/cross-tenant errors before a P0 hold, and TRE hold placement follows tenant/resource checks. A fresh independent re-review, full guarded suite, production build, and owner review remain required before a pull request can be opened.

The fresh adversarial re-review then found three further reachable score-authority paths outside the initial report and routing set. Automatic component-outcome learning had queried `claims.fraud_risk_score` and excluded scores at or above 50. That query and threshold are removed; learning inclusion now relies only on the explicit component-outcome evidence. Legacy TRE regulatory compliance had passed a CTO fraud score into regulatory thresholds that could force escalation or make an approval non-compliant. The compliance validator no longer accepts or evaluates a fraud score, and the legacy TRE hold now validates the session tenant and addressed claim before withholding its result. Finally, truth reconciliation added a score-labelled review trigger at 70 or above; the score trigger is removed so the P0-gated reconciliation wrapper supplies the standard actionable manual-review hold without embedding a raw fraud value.

New regressions prove that injected high and low historic fraud values produce identical regulatory compliance results, component learning source contains no fraud-score query or threshold guard, truth reconciliation emits no score-labelled trigger, and legacy TRE authorization precedes its hold. These repairs require one final fresh independent review before merge-readiness validation.

## Merged independent hardening baselines and explicit client limitation

This integration is rebased on the separately merged P0-B1 Independent Bypass Hardening, P0-B1 Independent Publication Hardening II, and P0-B1 Browser Fraud Publication Containment packages. It retains their canonical shared hold contract, server-side report/PDF/router guards, and six approved browser containment locations: four Claim Review displays and two Executive Dashboard alert-count displays.

This **does not constitute complete browser closure**. The evidence-backed client inventory identifies **45 additional reachable client files** with direct fraud-field references. They are explicitly deferred to the separately scoped **P0-B1-Client** package, which must be completed before P0-B2 begins. The P0-B1 merge record and any pull request must state this limitation plainly; the six-location containment is valuable, but it is not a substitute for systematic browser-surface remediation.
